## Overview

This article examines the representation of the Global South in Generative AI through a reproducible Monte Carlo simulation framework.

The study investigates the sampling uncertainty associated with reported colonial-gaze indicators in AI-generated image captions, addressing themes such as essentialism, cultural erasure, dehumanization, othering, and infantilization.

## Abstract

Generative AI systems can reproduce culturally uneven representations, while the categories used to describe the Global South may themselves carry homogenizing and imperial assumptions.

The study demonstrates a reproducible Monte Carlo framework for examining the sampling uncertainty of reported colonial-gaze indicators in AI-generated image captions.

Published proportions from a study involving more than 3,800 captions associated with 100 archival images were used as fixed reference values. Five themes were examined: essentialism, cultural erasure, dehumanization, othering, and infantilization.

Independent binomial samples of 100, 500, 1,000, and 3,800 captions were generated across 10,000 Monte Carlo simulations. Wilson 95% confidence intervals and several measures of statistical precision were evaluated.

The simulation results showed that mean proportions closely recovered the reference values under the assumed sampling model, while precision improved with increasing sample size.

An important limitation is that this simulation does not establish that contemporary AI models reproduce colonial representations at the same rates. The reference values are specific to the original study context and should not be generalized across all models, prompts, modalities, or Global South communities.
